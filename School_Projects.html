Some of my school projects at LUT University

The most memorable courses in Univeristy

I've been studying mechanical engineering at Lappeenranta Univeristy of Technology (LUT) for 4 years now and I've completed a total of 58 courses (45 courses for BSc; 13 for MSs) and have received a Bachelor's degree.

Below is a collage of some of the most useful or otherwise memorable courses I've had so far. The contents are roughly in order from newest to oldest. When hovering over the images/videos, you can scroll to see more content using the appearing red scrollbar.

3D modeling

For 3D modeling, I mainly use Solidworks for CAD models and Blender for anything that requires more "free flowing" shapes. I've experimented with some other softwares, but since I've been using SW and Blender the longest, those programs just feel the most natural to me.



Other Projects

I've been working on some silly things:

Advanced Additive Manufacturing

(COURSE STILL IN PROGRESS)
Our first task was to model a device that can separate water from compressed air. At first I made a three-part design, but that was rejected by the professor, so I made a new one-part design. Both models "should" work and they can be 3D-printed without major support structures.

Welding Automation

A really cool course, where we got to make a online welding program and weld parts with it in our university's welding laboratories. We also made a separate offline welding program in Visual Components OLP, that can be seen in the video/image. The video is 20 times faster than the intended run time.

Simulation of a Mechatronic Machine

A course with probably the best professor I've had so far. He made learning hard concepts actually fun AND understandable. The course also included simulating a hydraulic lifting boom system in MatLab Simulink (see images).

Principles of Industrial Manufacturing Processes

As one of the exercises, we made G-code for CNC-machining a part. We also had a group project where we took apart a machine of our choice, redesigned the parts, and thought of better manufacturing methods for the parts.
My part was the rotor.

Bachelor's Thesis

I did my bachelor's thesis about the application of collaborative robots (cobots) in welding production.

Like all theses, my thesis is public and you can read it on LUTPub.

Steel Structures

A course about strength analysis of different steel structures and welded joints. Here's two very short examples of the tasks we had to do. The first task (img. 1-3) uses rain flow method to calculate the equivalent force of a fatigue work cycle.
In the second task(img. 3-5) I calculated how many work cycles a welded joint should last.

Wind Power and Solar Energy Technology and Business

A course with a pretty fun task of planning a wind farm in a predetermined area in Finland. My area was Southern Ostrobothnia, and used Tuuliatlas to collect wind data to settle for the most optimal spot for my imaginary wind farm. We also had to determine what wind turbines to use and do some simple calculations on financing the wind farm.

Machine Design

As a group we designed a prototype for a modular oil purifier for a local business. From the images/video you can see the very first hand-drawn idea for the design and the final assembly. This was truly a rare group work project because everyone in the group did their part AND as best as they could.

FE-Analysis

Finite Element Analysis course, where we used mostly Femap to analyse different structures. The first image shows the translation of a structure and the second image shows Von Mises stresses affecting in a plate. The last image is from MatLab, and it's used to validate the results from Femap and to show how the number of elements affects the results.

Technical Design

A bit more extensive project made in a group, where we had to design a machine and build it. We made a trash compactor, and my resposibility was coming up with a support structure for our pneumatic cylinder.
From the images you can see an idea-matrix for different functions of the compactor and iterations of the design process for the cylinder support.

Strength Theory

We had two courses on strength theory. The first course had only pen-and-paper submissions for our assignments. The second course had a lot to do with standards and using ready-made calculation formulas. Thanks physicists!! The images (imgs. 2-4) show an example of dimensioning a rectangle tube for a seat.

Technical Documentation and 3D modeling

This course was my first introduction to 3D modeling and documentation. A good course, but the lackluster teaching and integration with academic Swedish studies made learning good 3D modeling and drawing practices a bit difficult.

Mechanics

We had at least two courses for basic (and in some case not so basic) mechanics calculations. The images are from a project where we got to decide a real construct to calculate the forces acting on the support structure. My construct was a stand for kayaks.
